## Provenance — why this is a RE-ARCHITECTURE, not "structured-schema attempt #4"

This proposal is the **standalone S-18 successor** to the superseded bundle [Proposal 016](016-s17-s18-persona-quality-lift.md). 016 bundled S-17 (renaissance) with S-18 (virtu). Across 016's three isolated-challenger rounds (R1/R2/R3, 2026-05-20/21) the **virtu half failed structurally** while renaissance carried zero structural items and shipped clean as [Proposal 017](017-s17-renaissance-backtesting-quality-lift.md).

The 016 virtu approach was a structured **quantitative cost schema** (`output_format` with `spread_cost_bps`, `commission_eur`, `commission_bps`, `total_cost_bps`, `pct_of_adv`). It was cut **6 → 6 → 4 blocks** across R1/R2/R3 and still did not converge. The reason — established by 016 R3-L6 (structural) plus the substantive cluster R3-L1/L2/L3 (verified, recorded verbatim in 016's Appendix) — is **not schema-tuning**:

- **R3-L2/L3** — `commission_eur` needs a broker fee schedule; `spread_cost_bps` needs a live order-book quote. Neither is in a War Room sub-agent's working context. `docs/DATA_STANDARDS.md` (read 2026-05-21) provisions price data (yfinance daily OHLCV, broker API), macro data (FRED/ECB), fundamentals — **no live spread feed and no broker fee schedule**. The same unsourceable-input test that killed `pct_of_adv` at R2 kills the rest of the cost block at R3. **(018 R3-L11 sharpening — the dividing line, stated precisely:)** it is the *exact* figure that is unsourceable, so a schema field demanding a precise number (`commission_eur: number`) is false precision; a *verdict* reasoned from a stable, publicly-known broker ballpark and *robust to that ballpark's imprecision* — the retail-order threshold call is unchanged whether the commission is at the low or high end of the broker's published range — is a structurally different object and IS fillable. "Precise datum unsourceable; ballpark-robust verdict fillable" is the actual dividing line; it does not contradict the broker-general-knowledge clarification in §"Why every field is fillable" — it is that clarification's basis.
- **R3-L6** (structural) — once the unfillable fields are removed, the schema shrinks to a MEDIUM `output_format` tidy. A quantitative cost schema **cannot** be the lift mechanism for virtu, because virtu's domain inputs are not available to the persona.

The same quantitative-schema mechanism **succeeded** for renaissance (017, composite 29 → 33) precisely because renaissance's schema inputs — rolling Sharpe, OOS ratio, turnover — come *from the backtest under review*, always present in the persona's working context. virtu's do not. **Input availability is the dividing line**, and it is why 018 must not reproduce-and-tweak the 016 virtu schema.

**018 is therefore a re-architecture of what the virtu persona *is*** — not a fourth attempt at a numeric `output_format`. The framing question 018 answers:

> *What can a Virtu execution persona output that is (a) firm-authentic, (b) fillable by a War Room sub-agent with NO live execution-data feed, and (c) genuinely useful to the desk's retail monthly-cadence DCA investor?*

The 016 R3 Challenger output (all 22 L-items) is preserved verbatim in 016's Appendix as **018 design input**. Every virtu-surface R3 item is addressed below and cross-referenced in the §Adversarial Loophole Pass.

**Root cause.** virtu's 29 was scored on 2026-05-15, *after* the S-16 D3 template was already applied (same date). The one-point gap therefore **cannot** be closed by the D3 template — it is already counted. The gap is **D6 (and D1)**: the persona is an institutional execution-algo designer dropped onto a retail monthly-DCA desk, and it cannot adapt because its mandate *is* the institutional algorithm.

**The fix is not a numeric `output_format` (016 proved that). The fix is to re-scope the persona to the desk's actual investor** and give it a structured output it can genuinely fill: reasoned **decision rules**, not measured costs. virtu has a real, useful retail surface — the four levers a retail monthly-DCA ETF buyer genuinely controls:

1. **Order type** — limit vs marketable-limit vs market. A plain market order on a less-liquid UCITS ETF cedes the spread for no benefit; a marketable limit caps it. Reasoned, no data feed needed.
2. **Timing** — avoid the open/close auctions and macro-release minutes (widest spreads). Reasoned rule on a fixed schedule.
3. **Commission dilution / consolidation** — a retail broker's fixed-minimum per-trade commission (the broker is named in `local/INVESTOR_PROFILE.md`; its published commission schedule is broker world-knowledge, not a desk data feed — R4-L64 corrected the v4 mis-cite that had attributed the figure to `INVESTOR_PROFILE.md` §Brokers itself) is on the order of 0.6% of a retail-scale monthly order. Consolidating several monthly contributions into one quarterly buy cuts it to roughly a third of that. Over decades this compounds. The strongest retail lever — and expressible as a *rule with a threshold*, not a sourced number.
4. **Fractional shares** — retail brokers commonly support fractional ETF orders; using them deploys the full contribution with no whole-share rounding cash drag.

All four are firm-authentic (Virtu Financial *is* an execution-quality / best-execution firm — that is its core brand), fillable with no live feed, and genuinely useful to the actual investor. This is the re-architecture.

## Proposal

### Architecture decision (HEAVY — ≥2 approaches; one MUST test "is a structured output_format the right mechanism at all")

> **Approach A — Retail re-scope + structured *decision-rule* `output_format`.** Re-scope the persona's identity to a retail execution-quality analyst; replace the free-prose `output_format` with a structured schema of *reasoned decision rules* (order-type rule, timing rule, cost-efficiency/consolidation rule, execution-quality rationale) — outputs the persona **reasons to**, not **measures**. — **Confidence 0.80 (single-author, pre-cross-check).**
> Pros: every field is fillable with no live feed (the only numerics are read from the trade plan under review, exactly as renaissance reads Sharpe from the backtest under review; the rule fields are conditional policies over instrument *types*, not measurements — see below); the structure forces all four retail levers to be addressed every time (the D1/D6 lift); breaks the static-label pair-diff lever vs jane_street/millennium (free-prose "...specification" → structured decision-rule); follows the validated citadel/pimco/renaissance free-prose → structured lift pattern.
> Cons: a decision-rule schema is a new pattern for this repo (citadel/pimco/renaissance all used measured-numeric schemas) — unproven here; risk it reads thin (mitigated — see §Adversarial Loophole Pass L2/L22/L25).
>
> **Approach B — Retail re-scope + another structured *numeric cost* `output_format`** (the 016 path: reproduce-and-tweak the cost schema). — **Confidence 0.20.**
> Pros: a measured-numeric schema is the proven repo pattern (citadel/pimco/renaissance).
> Cons: **this is the mechanism 016 R1/R2/R3 proved wrong for virtu.** R3-L2/L3 (verified): `commission_eur` and `spread_cost_bps` are not sourceable by a data-less War Room sub-agent. R3-L6 (structural): with the unfillable fields removed it shrinks to a MEDIUM tidy. Choosing B is repeating the failed path. **Rejected.**
>
> **Approach C — `system_prompt`-only re-scope; `output_format` stays free-prose** (lightly revised bullet list). The honestly-modest, no-new-architecture option. — **Confidence 0.45.**
> Pros: trivial; directly lifts D6 (the persona would now adapt to the retail context); would likely be a MEDIUM-tier change.
> Cons: leaves the `output_format` a free-prose blob. The eval failure mode for every sub-threshold persona in this repo (citadel pre-S-15 score 14; renaissance pre-S-17) was **the absence of enforced domain primitives** — a free-prose `output_format` enforces nothing. C lifts D6 but leaves D1/D3 without the structural enforcement that a schema provides, and leaves the static-label pair-diff lever (the shared free-prose "...specification" shape) intact vs jane_street/millennium. C might scrape ≥30 on a one-point gap — but it does not make the persona *coherent*, it just re-labels it.

A beats C because the structured decision-rule schema *enforces* the persona addresses all four retail levers every time (the D1/D6/D5 lift C cannot deliver) and breaks the pair-diff static label C leaves intact. The load-bearing lift is the **identity re-architecture** (the `system_prompt` re-scope); the decision-rule `output_format` is its **enforcement mechanism**. Together they are HEAVY.

### The re-architected persona

#### `output_format` — structured decision-rule schema (5 blocks)

```yaml
output_format:
  order_context:
    trades_this_session: number          # count of buy orders in the trade plan under review
    largest_order_eur: number            # EUR value of the largest single buy in the plan
    contribution_cadence: string         # from INVESTOR_PROFILE — the contribution amount and cadence
  order_type_rule:
    recommended_order_type: limit|marketable-limit|market
    rule: string                          # a CONDITIONAL decision rule (a policy over instrument
                                          # types, not a measurement of one) — ties order type to
                                          # the listing's liquidity TIER, a stable knowable
                                          # property (primary-listed large UCITS ETF vs thin
                                          # secondary listing); never to a live spread number
    market_order_caveat: string           # the explicit condition under which a plain market
                                          # order is acceptable, or a statement that it is not
  timing_rule:
    placement_window: string              # reasoned rule — when in the session to place
    avoid: string                         # explicit windows to avoid (open/close auctions,
                                          # macro-release minutes — fixed-schedule, no feed
                                          # needed) and the reason
  cost_efficiency:
    fixed_commission_known_by: investor-broker-statement   # documents that the EXACT per-trade
                                          # commission is supplied by the investor from their own
                                          # broker confirmation (the behavioural rule is in
                                          # system_prompt constraint (ii) — not this comment)
    consolidation_trigger: string         # the reasoned trigger rule — e.g. "if the broker's
                                          # fixed per-trade commission exceeds 0.5% of the order
                                          # value, consolidate N monthly contributions into one"
    consolidation_verdict: efficient|consolidate-recommended|investor-must-check
    fractional_share_rule: string         # reasoned rule on using fractional ETF orders to
                                          # deploy the full contribution (no rounding cash drag)
  execution_quality_rationale: string     # WHY this order type + timing minimise the investor's
                                          # OWN execution cost — reasoning, not a citation. May
                                          # reference MiFID II Art.27 ONLY to note venue routing
                                          # is the broker's delegated best-execution duty.
```

**Why every field is fillable with no live feed** (closes the R3-L2/L3 unsourceable-input failure; R1-L1 + R2-L4 sharpening applied):
- `order_context` numerics (`trades_this_session`, `largest_order_eur`) are read from the **trade plan under review** — the War Room Phase 6 trade-plan table carries shares, price, and cost per order. This is exactly how renaissance reads `in_sample_sharpe` from the backtest under review. The trade plan *is* in the persona's working context; a broker fee schedule and a live order book are not.
- `contribution_cadence` is read from `local/INVESTOR_PROFILE.md` §Investment Constraints (the contribution amount and cadence).
- The `*_rule` strings and `consolidation_trigger` are **conditional decision rules** — *policies over instrument types and fixed schedules*, not measurements. `order_type_rule.rule` keys off the listing's **liquidity tier** (a stable, knowable property — a primary-listed large UCITS ETF vs a thin secondary listing — derivable from the instrument named in the trade plan), **not** a live spread number. `timing_rule` keys off fixed session events (the open/close auctions are at fixed times every day). The persona *reasons* these rules; it does not *source* a datapoint.
- **Broker general knowledge vs a data feed (R2-L4).** The `consolidation_verdict` is grounded by the persona's *general knowledge of the named broker* — `INVESTOR_PROFILE` names the broker, and a retail broker's fixed-minimum commission is a stable, publicly-known ballpark, world knowledge the persona already holds — **not** a live feed and **not** a per-instrument fee schedule. The persona uses this ballpark to reason the verdict for a standard order, and flags the investor to confirm the *exact* figure on their own broker confirmation (that exact figure is what `fixed_commission_known_by: investor-broker-statement` documents). `consolidation_verdict: investor-must-check` is reserved for genuinely boundary or unknown-broker cases — it is **not** a default hedge, and for a standard retail monthly buy the persona has enough to reason a definite verdict (DoD #18).
- No `commission_eur`, `commission_bps`, `spread_cost_bps`, `total_cost_bps`, or `pct_of_adv` field exists, and **the implemented schema's field set must equal the specification above exactly** — no extra fields (DoD #1's positive exact-schema-match is the structural guard; DoD #2's negative grep is a secondary check — R2-L3).

`recommended_order_type` and `consolidation_verdict` are enum scalars (precedent-consistent with citadel's `category:` and renaissance's `overfitting_verdict:`). `consolidation_verdict` is genuinely variable across order sizes (small monthly → `consolidate-recommended`; consolidated/large → `efficient`; genuinely boundary/unknown → `investor-must-check`).

#### `system_prompt` — re-scope (the load-bearing lift)

Rewritten so the persona is a **retail execution-quality analyst at Virtu Financial advising the desk's retail monthly-cadence DCA investor** — the contribution amount and the broker name are read from `local/INVESTOR_PROFILE.md` at runtime, never hardcoded into this tracked file (2026-05-21 de-hardcode amendment). It:
- Mandates the 5-block decision-rule schema; **removes** the legacy free-prose "execution algorithm specification" output mandate and the institutional TWAP/VWAP/implementation-shortfall-optimiser/iceberg/smart-order-routing/market-impact-model design mandate.
- Carries four constraint paragraphs (all behavioural MUSTs in `system_prompt`, never in YAML comments — closes R3-L12 / R1-L11):
  - **(i) Retail-scale honesty** — at the desk's retail monthly-DCA order sizes, market impact is effectively zero; the genuine levers are order type, timing, fixed-commission dilution, and fractional deployment. Do not design institutional execution algorithms.
  - **(ii) Data-availability honesty** — the desk has no live spread feed and no broker fee schedule. You reason *rules* — conditional policies over instrument liquidity *tiers* and fixed session schedules — you do not measure costs. You may reason with *general knowledge of the investor's broker* (named in `local/INVESTOR_PROFILE.md`) — a retail broker's fixed-minimum commission is a stable, publicly-known ballpark; you must flag it as a ballpark and direct the investor to confirm the exact figure on their broker confirmation. Where a verdict genuinely depends on a commission you cannot ballpark, output `investor-must-check` — but do not use `investor-must-check` as a default hedge for a standard order on a known retail broker.
  - **(iii) Upstream-instrument boundary** — the instrument and its listing are chosen upstream (the D.E. Shaw structural-selection step). You do **not** re-select the listing or analyse share-class liquidity asymmetry. Where the listing's liquidity tier enters your reasoning, it is solely as an input to the order-type decision, **not** as a listing-selection verdict. (Closes R3-L5 — the de_shaw spread overlap, now explicitly disclaimed.)
  - **(iv) Execution-quality reasoning** — `execution_quality_rationale` must state *why* the chosen order type and timing minimise the investor's **own** execution cost. It may reference MiFID II Article 27 **only** to note that venue routing is the *broker's* delegated best-execution duty (Article 27 binds the investment firm, not the retail self-directed client — R2-L6); it must not frame the investor's own order-type decision as the discharge of a regulatory obligation.
- Carries the consolidation-threshold rule: commission > 0.5% of order value → consolidate; small monthly contributions on a fixed-minimum-commission broker typically breach the trigger and warrant `consolidate-recommended`.
- Required vocabulary: "execution quality", "limit order", "marketable limit", "commission drag", "implementation shortfall" (the decision-vs-fill price gap — a valid concept at retail scale), "contribution consolidation", "fractional shares". (MiFID II Article 27 is *permitted* in the narrow venue-delegation sense above but is **not** required vocabulary — R2-L6.)
- Ends with a vocabulary-discipline block containing a required-vocabulary line and a line **beginning the literal string `- Forbidden phrases`** (the renaissance/017 format — `renaissance_backtesting.yml:84` is the precedent; DoD #4 and #8 anchor their greps to this exact line, so the spec mandates the line exists — R2-L8). **Drafting note (R3-L5/L12):** virtu's retail-broker domain sits adjacent to `millennium_live_trading`'s "broker API integration" — the implementation must phrase broker references (broker confirmation, broker commission, fractional orders placed via the broker) without the forbidden phrase, and must keep the forbidden-phrase content on the single declaration line (DoD #4/#8 anchor on exactly one such line). Flagged for the implementation-agent so a careless phrasing does not trip DoD #8. **(R4-L63):** likewise, DoD #3 requires the bare phrase "market impact" inside constraint (i) ("market impact is effectively zero"), while DoD #4 forbids the 3-word institutional term "market impact model" outside the `- Forbidden phrases` line — the bare 2-word phrase in constraint (i) is permitted; only the 3-word phrase is forbidden. The implementation-agent must keep that distinction so the two greps do not collide.

#### `capabilities` — rewrite (closes R3-L15)

The current `capabilities` list advertises institutional functions ("TWAP and VWAP algorithm design", "Iceberg order logic", "Smart order routing", "Slippage measurement and market impact modeling") the re-architected persona repudiates. Rewritten to the retail surface: retail order-type selection, monthly-DCA timing discipline, fixed-commission dilution and contribution-consolidation analysis, fractional-share deployment, execution-quality reasoning, retail implementation-shortfall awareness.

#### `review_prompt` — coherence reframe (NOT a quality rewrite)

virtu's `review_prompt` is already post-010 quality (adversarial frame + 4 numbered checks + S-16 actionability template + effort-estimate clause) — it is **not** the lift lever. But its four checks reference institutional execution (market impact on "actual liquidity", dark pools, TWAP/VWAP schedule risk). For the re-architected persona to be internally coherent, the checks are **reframed** to the four retail levers — structure, adversarial posture, actionability template, and the `< 1 day / 1-3 days / > 1 week` effort-estimate clause all **preserved verbatim**:

New adversarial frame: *"Assume this monthly buy quietly bleeds the edge — the full spread is paid every month, the commission is a heavy percentage of a small order, and the timing is careless — and that small avoidable costs compound against a DCA investor over decades. Find where the edge leaks."*

Four reframed checks (the four check *names* are committed verbatim and asserted by DoD #6): (1) **Order-Type Discipline** — is a plain market order used where a marketable limit would cap the spread? Is order type matched to listing liquidity tier? (2) **Timing** — does the plan place orders into the open/close auction or a macro-release window? (3) **Commission Drag & Consolidation** — is the fixed per-trade commission a material % of the order value? Would consolidating contributions cut it materially? (4) **Execution Quality & Fractional Deployment** — does the rationale give a correct causal reason *why* the order type + timing minimise the investor's own execution cost (not merely cite a regulation)? Is the full contribution deployed via fractional shares, or is rounding cash drag left on the table? "Do not rubber-stamp" + the S-16 actionability template + the effort-estimate clause retained verbatim.

The "costs compound over decades" second-order frame is the substantive source of the persona's retail adversarial depth (R1-L8 — see L20/L28): a small avoidable per-order cost compounding across a 10+-year DCA horizon is a real, non-obvious failure mode a competent retail-execution review surfaces.
